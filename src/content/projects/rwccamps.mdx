Somewhere along the way, I became the default school-days-off planner for our family. Every school break or teacher workday meant cross-referencing calendars, opening a dozen tabs, and piecing together camps that worked for our schedules, budgets, and kids. As a parent of two and a product designer with little patience for unnecessarily complicated experiences, I decided to build the tool I wished existed.

In building [RWC Camps](https://rwccamps.com/), I've been thinking a lot about search, particularly what it could look like in the AI era. Parents aren't simply looking for camps. They're solving for childcare, juggling schedules, managing budgets, finding enriching experiences, and coordinating with other parents so their kids can spend time with friends. What would search look like if we designed around what people are trying to accomplish rather than just what they're trying to find?

[RWC Camps](https://rwccamps.com/) is my little playground for exploring these questions while building something useful for the community I call home. The hope is to make planning feel a little lighter, so parents can spend less time planning for their kids and more time being with them.
